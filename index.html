The Premise

Hair itself cannot be treated. Its root can.

The hair shaft you see is, biologically, dead tissue. No cream or serum can bring it back to life. The only part that's still alive is the follicle beneath the scalp — and that's the only place worth caring for.

When the scalp is clogged with synthetic residue from commercial shampoos — silicone, sulfates, layers of pomade — the pore closes, and the follicle pushes out a thinner strand with every cycle.

Hair doesn't fall out because the strand is weak. It falls because its grip has loosened.

Hair Care

Four things that
damage your hair
every single day.

It's usually not the product that's the problem. Unnoticed daily habits do far more damage. These are the four we see most often.

Room 01 — Mechanical Tension

Tight buns & hijab inner caps

Pulling your hair into a tight bun for eight hours means every strand is under constant tension for eight hours straight. The root never gets to rest. A tight inner cap rubs against the hairline, so the baby hairs there go first — which is why the hairline can look like it's receding even when the rest of the scalp is still thick.

How to fix it
  • Swap regular elastics for a satin scrunchie. A plain elastic grips a single point
  • Tie it loose. If it pulls on your hairline skin, it's too tight
  • Shift your part every few days
  • Apply serum along the hairline every night
Room 02 — Thermal Trauma

Hot hairdryer on dripping-wet hair

Water trapped inside the hair shaft boils when hit with direct hot air. The steam gets trapped and bursts outward, leaving microscopic bubbles along the strand — a condition called bubble hair. The hair becomes brittle, snaps in the middle, and splits at the ends even right after a fresh cut.

How to fix it
  • Towel-dry first until it stops dripping. Don't rub — press it instead
  • Use the cool setting. Slower, but it won't damage the hair
  • If you need heat, keep it a palm's width away and keep it moving
  • Finish with a cool blast — it closes the cuticle, so the hair looks shinier
Room 03 — Waterlogged Ground

Brushing hair right out of the shower

Wet hair swells and softens — its tensile strength is at its lowest point right then. The scalp is much the same, like waterlogged ground after rain. Brushing from root to tip at this moment means you're pulling out strands that are actually still perfectly healthy.

How to fix it
  • Wait until it's roughly 70% dry
  • Use a wide-tooth comb, not a fine-bristle brush
  • Start at the ends, working your way up gradually
  • When you hit a knot, work it loose with your fingers — don't force the comb through
Room 04 — The Greenhouse Effect

Helmets, sweat and pomade residue

Our climate is hot and humid. A helmet seals everything in, sweat never gets the chance to dry, and the scalp sits in trapped heat for the entire ride. Mix in leftover pomade or wax that wasn't fully rinsed out, and the pore closes up tight, dandruff thickens, and the scalp smells even with a daily shower.

How to fix it
  • Wash it the same day if you've worn wax or pomade
  • Air-dry your helmet padding — it holds onto sweat and bacteria
  • Make sure hair is fully dry before putting the helmet on
  • The peppermint in our shampoo cools the scalp down after a ride

Key Ingredient

Rosemary.
The most evidence-backed
ingredient for the scalp.

Of all the botanical ingredients used for hair, rosemary has the strongest body of clinical research behind it. We didn't choose it because it's trending.

Why Rosemary

It works at the root, not on the surface.

Rosemary works through three pathways at once. It dilates the fine blood vessels in the scalp, increasing perfusion to the follicle. It's anti-inflammatory, calming a scalp irritated by dandruff or eczema. And the rosmarinic acid within it acts as an antioxidant that protects the follicle from oxidative stress.

A clinical comparison study found rosemary oil performed on par with a conventional topical treatment in results after six months — with a lower rate of scalp itching.

We pair it with peppermint because the two work in tandem: rosemary dilates, peppermint cools and opens the pore. In a tropical climate, that combination isn't a style choice. It's a necessity.

Ingredient Specification

What goes in, and why.

Rosmarinus officinalis Steam-distilled essential oil. Dilates scalp blood vessels, anti-inflammatory, antioxidant. The primary active in both products.
Mentha piperita Natural menthol. Delivers a cooling effect, opens the pore, and brings down scalp temperature after it's been trapped under a helmet or hijab.
Caprylic/Capric Triglyceride (MCT) The base oil for the serum. Its molecule is small, absorbs without leaving a greasy film, and doesn't clog the pore.
Botanical Glucoside A gentle surfactant derived from sugar and coconut. It cleanses without stripping the scalp's protective lipid barrier — unlike the SLS found in commercial shampoos.
Citric Acid Brings the formula's pH down into a healthy range for the scalp, helping the cuticle close tightly after washing.
Preservative — shampoo only Benzyl alcohol and dehydroacetic acid, at a minimal rate. Approved for use in certified-organic natural products. The shampoo contains water, and water without a preservative breeds bacteria within days. Anyone selling a water-based shampoo as "preservative-free" is selling something unsafe, or not telling the truth.
What's not in it Sulfates (SLS/SLES), silicone, parabens, synthetic fragrance, dye, mineral oil.
Shelf Life

How long it lasts.

Serum — unopened 24 months from the batch date. This serum is anhydrous — 100% oil, not a single drop of water. Bacteria, mould and yeast all need water to survive. Without water, the formula is self-stable. That's why it contains no preservative, and needs none.
Serum — after opening 12 months. What limits it isn't microbes, but oxidation. Every time it's opened, oxygen gets in. The amber glass protects against light; keep it away from windows and the bathroom, and it stays fresh for that whole period.
Shampoo — unopened 18 months from the batch date.
Shampoo — after opening 9 months. It's a pump bottle, not a wide-mouth jar — your fingers never touch the inside, so no bacteria gets introduced with every use.
Storage Room temperature, away from direct sunlight. Avoid storing in the bathroom if you can — heat and humidity speed up oxidation in both products.
Batch dating Every bottle is labeled with a batch number and the date it was formulated. We make in small quantities so no stock sits on a shelf for long before it reaches you.
